The analysis focused on **identifying revenue erosion by cross-referencing sales transactions with return records**. While the overall store performance is strong, certain sub-categories are experiencing high return frequencies that significantly impact net profitability.

1️⃣ 𝗛𝗶𝗴𝗵-𝗥𝗲𝘁𝘂𝗿𝗻 𝗦𝘂𝗯-𝗖𝗮𝘁𝗲𝗴𝗼𝗿𝗶𝗲𝘀 𝗮𝗻𝗱 𝗣𝗿𝗼𝗳𝗶𝘁 𝗘𝗿𝗼𝘀𝗶𝗼𝗻 <br>
- The **Machines sub-category has emerged as the highest-risk area with a return rate of 11.35%, followed closely by Tables (9.49%) and Paper (9.20%)**. The Machines sub-category is currently the only segment exceeding the 10% management threshold.
- Returns for **Binders and Tables show a negative profit impact (-$5,454.91 and -$1,757.77, respectively)**. This indicates a double-loss scenario. The store is using heavy discounts (loss-leaders) to move these products, but when they are returned, **the store absorbs the initial loss plus the operational cost of the return.** This suggests these sub-categories are currently a drain on regional resources.
- While Copiers have a lower return rate (6.41%), their individual price points are high. **Even with a lower frequency, returned copiers account for $8,610.94 in profit erosion — the highest in the store.**

2️⃣ 𝗥𝗲𝗴𝗶𝗼𝗻𝗮𝗹 𝗩𝗮𝗿𝗶𝗮𝗻𝗰𝗲 𝗔𝗻𝗮𝗹𝘆𝘀𝗶𝘀
- The West Region, managed by Anna Andreadi, is currently facing a critical return crisis: **the return rate in the West stands at 20%, which is more than double the company-wide average and significantly higher than the 10% management threshold.**
- Managed by Chuck Magee, the East Region is exceeding the acceptable threshold. **While not as severe as the West, the East is still seeing a high level of churn (a return rate of 13.33%).**
- Kelly Williams in the Central Region is currently the top performer regarding return mitigation (an exceptionally low return rate of 3.57%). **The Central Region provides a benchmark for the rest of the company.**
- **A mandatory operational audit is recommended for the West Region.** We need to investigate whether the high return rate is due to a specific warehouse’s shipping errors, an aggressive sales culture that over-promises on product capabilities, or a high concentration of the "Machines" sub-category returns in that area.

<br>

3️⃣ 𝗦𝗵𝗶𝗽𝗽𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗗𝘂𝗿𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝗖𝗼𝗿𝗿𝗲𝗹𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻
- This analysis investigated **the relationship between operational speed** - measured as days to ship (the duration between order and shipment) - and **customer behavior, specifically the return rate**.
- The objective was **to determine if logistical delays are a primary driver of returns or if other operational factors are at play.**
- The most striking finding is that **Same Day (0-day) shipping has the highest return rate in the entire dataset at 13.84%.** Orders shipped the same day they are placed **are significantly more likely to be returned compared to orders with a 2–5-day window**.
- Shipping durations of 2 days and 5 days show the lowest return rates **(7.20% and 6.10%, respectively)**.
- We need to **investigate the packing process for Same-Day shipments.** We should implement a mandatory "double-check" step for these orders to ensure that speed is not sacrificing accuracy.
